# Sharing changes with your care team

AI-authored draft. Source-faithfulness and clinical review pending.

Lesson: `psa-contact-01` · Topic: `contact` · About 2 minutes

## Page 1

Tell the care team about symptoms that are new, keep returning, or are getting worse. A body map can help you name the area: skin, nails, hands, wrists, shoulders, elbows, back, hips, knees, ankles, or feet. Mention fatigue and changes in walking, sleep, work, or self-care. A useful message says where the problem is, when it began, and what has changed. You can say, for example, that new knee pain makes stairs harder.

## Page 2

A body map cannot tell you how urgent a symptom is. The next lesson explains eye and gut changes to report. Medicine concerns and repeated flares are also reasons to contact the prescribing team. Ask your clinic for its phone number and office hours. Ask about expected response times and how to get help when it is closed. Keep your own care plan available. For urgent symptoms, use urgent medical services rather than waiting for a routine app message.

## Key takeaways

- A useful message says where the problem is, when it began, and what has changed.
- A body map cannot tell you how urgent a symptom is.
