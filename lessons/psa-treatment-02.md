The management chapter describes several broad treatment categories used across inflammatory arthritis and autoimmune disease.

Conventional synthetic DMARDs, or csDMARDs, are disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs made as traditional small-molecule medicines. The chapter describes them as an early form of disease-modifying treatment.

Biologic therapies are engineered proteins designed to block specific signaling molecules or cell targets. The source explains that the biologic era expanded from TNF to other pathways and specifically notes the IL-17 and IL-23 pathway in psoriatic arthritis.

Targeted synthetic DMARDs, or tsDMARDs, are small-molecule medicines aimed at specific signaling systems. JAK means Janus kinase, and TYK2 means tyrosine kinase 2. The chapter describes JAK inhibitors as working at signaling points inside cells and TYK2-selective therapy as acting on pathways relevant to psoriasis and PsA.

These are treatment classes, not instructions about which medicine a person should use.
