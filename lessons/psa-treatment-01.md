The PsA one-pager says to assess every GRAPPA domain and choose therapy that covers as many active domains as possible. The reason is that different PsA patterns do not always respond to the same treatment class.

For peripheral arthritis, the source emphasizes treating the joints to a target. A DMARD is a disease-modifying antirheumatic drug. For axial disease, it notes that conventional synthetic DMARDs, or csDMARDs, do not treat the spine and lists other treatment classes used for axial disease. For enthesitis and dactylitis, the source says these findings can influence selection of biologic or targeted synthetic DMARD classes.

Skin and nail psoriasis can also steer treatment choice. The one-pager links these domains with cytokine-targeting classes such as IL-17, IL-23, and TNF inhibitors. IL means interleukin, and TNF means tumor necrosis factor. Both are cytokines, which are signaling proteins used by immune cells.

This lesson describes the organization shown in the source and does not rank medicines or recommend an individual treatment.
