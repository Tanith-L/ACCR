The GRAPPA poster groups PsA into six core treatment domains. A domain is a major pattern of disease that can be active on its own or with other patterns.

- Peripheral arthritis: inflammatory synovitis of joints in the arms or legs. Synovitis means inflammation of a joint lining.
- Axial disease: inflammation of the spine and sacroiliac joints, which can cause inflammatory back pain and stiffness.
- Enthesitis: inflammation where tendons or ligaments attach to bone, commonly at the Achilles tendon or plantar fascia.
- Dactylitis: swelling of a whole finger or toe from joint, tendon-sheath, and soft-tissue inflammation.
- Skin psoriasis: well-defined red plaques with silvery scale.
- Nail psoriasis: changes such as pitting, onycholysis, oil-drop change, or thickening under the nail. Onycholysis means the nail separates from the nail bed.
