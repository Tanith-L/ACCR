The Four Pillars chapter calls movement the second pillar. It discusses resistance or isotonic exercise, Tai Chi, yoga, Pilates, cycling, and water-based movement as examples studied in arthritis. Isotonic exercise means a muscle changes length while working against resistance.

The chapter presents movement as a way to support strength, function, balance, and confidence. Its examples come from different arthritis populations, so they are not presented here as a PsA-specific exercise prescription.

The third pillar is called mastery and focuses on the mind. The chapter discusses mindfulness, meditation, prayer, and other reflective practices in relation to pain, stress, attention, and coping. It describes the mind as influencing how a flare is interpreted and how a person continues other health-supporting routines.

These are educational concepts from the source, not individualized instructions.
